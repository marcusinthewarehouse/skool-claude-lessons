## Summary

Hey everyone! Tonight's Think Tank turned into a full-on business strategy session. Less code, more "how do you actually make money with this stuff." Some of the best practical advice we've had on a call in a while - especially from David, Eric, Erica, and Rodrigo. Here's the rundown:

## Josh's Boutique AI Consultancy - $10K Audits as Paid Discovery

- Josh fully left his IT company last week and is now running a boutique AI consultancy. Already sold two $10K audits, including one to a 100-person nonprofit and one to a real estate loan syndication shop.
- Both deals came from warm intros that sat dormant for ~6 months. The unlock was having an offer and deliverable ready when they were finally ready to move.
- Pitch: don't start by buying tools or automating. Start at the very beginning, understand how the company actually works, build an ROI-backed roadmap. He has 150+ workflows pulled out of interviews so far.
- Goal is $10-15K MRR on the implementation side after the audit. The audit is paid discovery - the real money is in what comes next.

## Riches Are in the Niches - Own Your IP, Resell It

- David's big one: put a clause in your contracts that you retain ownership and the right to resell the work. Clients don't read contracts. Once you've absorbed industry knowledge from a niche, that knowledge is gold - turn it into repeatable, productized work.
- $10K contracts are not a fun life - you need 24 a year to make real money. Use the audit to learn the niche, then resell the framework across the industry.
- Rodrigo dropped his actual IP clause from his contracts in the chat (Clause 8 - Intellectual Property). Worth grabbing if you don't have one.
- 10% of a workflow is AI / API / LLM. The other 90% is plain automation and scripts - but that 10% is where the value gets added.

## Become a SaaS Partner - Eric's "Boring" Goldmine

- Eric runs AI implementation for Zendesk and said if he wasn't doing that, he'd 100% be a partner for the big SaaS companies - AWS, Five9, ServiceNow, Zendesk, etc. His friend who does this makes silly money.
- How it works: apply to the partner program (AWS, Five9, etc.), go through their training, get the stamp. Once approved you can resell, implement, and they refer customers too small for them down to you.
- Use the partner work as a door - get in doing the boring AWS Amazon Connect build, then upsell your own AI system on top once you've built trust.
- Links Eric dropped: aws.amazon.com/partners and five9.com/partners.
- Rodrigo's tip: if you've got a SaaS, get listed in GCP / AWS partner directories. Their portfolio companies get cloud credits they can spend on certified partners - free pipeline.

## Training Corporate Teams on the AI They Already Pay For

- Eric kept hammering this one in chat: every big company already pays for Claude, OpenAI, Cursor, Copilot, Gemini. Only engineering uses them well. Sales, accounting, account managers don't know what to do beyond "make this email professional."
- Massive underserved niche: go into corporate, train non-technical teams on the AI tools their company already pays for. Erica's example - her old healthcare company paid $250K for three people to come in for two weeks to do implementation training across departments.
- This is the play Erica thinks fits me best - solutions architect with bespoke team training, not generic AI consulting.

## Education vs Done-For-You - Pick One Lane

- I asked the group whether to build a consulting offer around education or done-for-you implementation. Mixed answers, but the strongest framing came from Ben: don't do both for the same client - creates conflict (they'll learn enough to second-guess your model choices and prices).
- Erica's take: doing the doing is the unsaturated lane. People want someone to just build it. Consulting/strategy is way more saturated. Better to be a solutions architect than a fractional CTO.
- Rodrigo: education works best as a funnel into your SaaS or retainer - teach them what they don't have, then sell them your infrastructure.

## Getting Your First Client - Do It For Free, Then Use the Network

- David's honest answer when Draper asked how to land your first client: do it for fucking free. Find someone successful and well-networked, get proximity (pay for it if you have to - join the mastermind), deliver one win, then ask "who else in your niche could use this?"
- Erica's introvert version: pick a specific niche (bookkeepers, coaches, accountants), go where they hang out (Facebook groups, Skool groups, Reddit), and serve - not sell. Drop genuine value publicly. Have a Calendly link in your profile (no website needed). Profiles do more selling than DMs.
- Always end public posts with "shoot me a message if you need help." Lowers the friction massively. David still gets DMs years later from old posts.
- Networking move from David: at events, find the person hiding by the wall, befriend them first. Then introduce new arrivals to the people they want to meet - become the connector. People remember the introducer way more than the talker.
- Referral program tip from Erica: just ask. She paid $500 per new retainer client referred. Generated more demand than she could handle.

## Upwork as a Strategy (or Not)

- Erica is a top 1% Upwork freelancer. Her teaching: pros come in trying to charge $150/hr right away and get crushed. You have to grind the bottom for badges, social proof, and job success score, then walk your rate up fast.
- Once your profile is built, enterprise clients on Upwork will pay real money and won't treat you like bottom-feeder labor. But you can't skip the proof-building phase.
- Erica and I are cross-pollinating communities - she'll do a freelancing-with-AI crash course here soon, and I'll do an AI monetization course in her community. Watch for the calendar.

## Productizing Cortex - State of the Plan

- Ben asked about reselling Cortex. Honest answer: I haven't fully productized it yet. Right now the community is the product. Resellers (like David) are figuring it out client by client.
- David's pitch in property management: "this is rough, you get your own Mac mini, get your nephew involved, but you get to keep your data and you talk to Telegram from the golf course." Owns the maintenance department from his phone. Sells the lifestyle, not the tech.
- Where I want to take it: cloud-based Cortex where multiple employees from the same company log into a shared instance with their own agents that collaborate. If you want to dedicate real time to building this with me, DM me - I'm assembling a small group.

## Using AI as a Manager (Eric's Real Problem)

- Eric manages 27 people on Slack and asked the question I think a lot of you have - how do I actually use AI as a manager, not just as a coder?
- The setup I described: one Cortex agent that has your playbooks, SOPs, and management style baked in. Plug it into Slack. Team members message "Eric 2.0" first - it answers anything that doesn't need the real Eric, escalates the rest.
- Plus a coaching agent that pulls Gong call transcripts and surfaces negative sentiment, who's struggling, who needs a check-in. Build a CRM-style profile per direct report - timeline of what they're working on, what was discussed in the last 1:1, what their measurables are.
- Best frame: present solutions not problems. The agent gives you three options with its opinion for any decision you don't have domain expertise on.

## Legal and Security in Enterprise

- Draper raised the legal angle on cloning people's personas, voice prints, transcript databases. Honest answer: laws haven't caught up. EU has stricter data rules. US is mostly the Wild West for now.
- Real risk isn't legal - it's security. Agents are stochastic systems with access to Slack, task managers, employee data. One bad day from a serious incident in someone's enterprise environment.
- Draper (security engineer at a 30K-person company) said it bluntly: the security teams are 10 years behind. Things are happening through API keys and 443 access whether IT approves or not. Disaster waiting on the calendar.

## Tool Drop: Postiz for Cross-Platform Posting

- Draper shared Postiz (github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app) - open-source, self-hostable cross-platform social scheduler. Same model as n8n. If you've been wanting to live-stream or post across TikTok / Instagram / YouTube at once, this is the one to test.
- Timing matters as much as content. Postiz + your AI agent figuring out optimal slot per audience = real reach amplification. Be ready for the 90-day consistency grind though.

## The David Hunter Daily Text Habit

- David texts three people every day: "you just popped on my mind, wanted to check in." That's it. Nobody does this and it blew my mind on the call.
- If you have a personal CRM (I do, and recommend building one), this becomes trivial to run as a daily habit. Network maintenance with near-zero friction.
