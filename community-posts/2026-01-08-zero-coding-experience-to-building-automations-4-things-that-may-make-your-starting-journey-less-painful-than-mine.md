From “ChatGPT basic usage” to running my own automation server in 6 weeks - here’s what I learned

Got a DM this morning that made me realize something: there’s a gap in the “first steps” content out there for people wanting to level up with AI.

The question was basically: “How did you go from basic usage to building hardcore stuff? Where do I even start?”
Now let me be clear—the pain is the journey. That’s how you actually learn this stuff. But some of my pain was just me not knowing what I didn’t know, and that’s the part I can maybe help you skip.

Here’s the thing I’ve realized: the simple how-to stuff is literally what AI is for. Just ask it. Tell it “explain this like I’m a beginner” and let it walk you through step by step. Copy code, paste it, see if it works. That’s how you get your feet wet.

But the next-level insights—the stuff you wouldn’t even think to ask—that’s where community comes in. AI will happily agree with you that it’s amazing, but it won’t proactively suggest the tricks that actually change your workflow.

So here’s what I’ve learned in 6 weeks:

1.	Claude Code was the first big leap. Instead of me debugging everything manually\(copy and paste from chat to terminal\), I give it the prompt and it tests, debugs, and comes back with questions. Massive time-saver.
2.	You can skip tools like n8n entirely. Shoutout to James for this one—realizing I could just run everything on my own server instead of wrestling with external trigger systems was huge.
3.	Claude Desktop sped everything up because it interacts directly with local files. Everything got more efficient.
4.	Prompt mindfulness is key. And look—I have ZERO coding background and I still go in circles. I’ve literally built and debugged the same thing like 7 times now. WTF. 😅 It’s part of the process. You’ll think you’re being crystal clear and still end up somewhere weird. Just keep going.

The simple stuff? Just ask AI and play around.

The bigger, more surprising ideas? That’s what feel like James is building here, and it’s been invaluable.
