My wife and I have a 60-acre ranch in southern Colorado, and I built her a 24/7 YouTube livestream so anyone can tune in and watch the scenery in real time. There are two camera feeds a PTZ camera that pans around the property showing the mountains, pastures, and dome chicken coop, and a birdfeeder cam that catches all the birds and wildlife that come through. The PTZ camera automatically rotates through different views so there's always something new to look at.The whole system runs on a laptop at the ranch with a Python app I built that manages the YouTube broadcasts, handles the camera feeds, and automatically restarts the streams to stay within YouTube's limits. There's a web dashboard I can check from anywhere to make sure everything's running. It also captures timelapse footage automatically every day. The system takes a snapshot every minute from 4am to 10pm, so it catches everything from first light to sunset. At midnight it compiles all the frames from that day into a timelapse video usually around 1,000+ frames condensed into about 30 seconds at 30fps. It keeps a rolling week of timelapses so we always have recent footage to look back on. I even set it up so she can post the timelapse and bird videos directly from the dashboard. They can also be qeued up to schedule video posts later throughout the week to the channel. Some of the sunrise and sunset compilations have been incredible. It started as a fun project to share the ranch with friends and family but it's turned into something pretty cool — a live window into rural Colorado life, 24/7.
