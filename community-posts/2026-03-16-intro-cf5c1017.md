Hey Team!

My name is Rocky — I'm 28, based in St. George, Utah, and I run an AI development studio called Haestus.

For most of my career, I was in sales. About 2.5 years ago, I started playing with ChatGPT — just conversations, asking questions, nothing serious. But I got hooked. I followed the AI space closely and kept hearing about this company called Anthropic. I researched their mission, their models, and started going deep. I eventually downgraded my $200 ChatGPT plan to $20 and went all-in on Claude. Best decision I've made. I've been building in Claude Code ever since, and my perspective on what's possible has completely shifted.

Here's the thing about the AI space right now — social media is flooded with gurus, courses, and noise. "Market disruption." "The next million-dollar AI tool just dropped." You know the drill. It's exhausting. That's exactly why James stood out to me. I came across Agentic James early on, and he was different. He wasn't optimizing for clicks or attention — he was leading with value. He was speaking the language of someone who actually builds, not someone who just talks about building.
I reached out to him directly, and even before he had a course, he was willing to help and have real conversations. When he finally released the course, I was all in from day one.

I want to be honest about what this journey has done for me: I'm now generating tens of thousands of dollars a month building AI solutions for businesses. That's real. But I also want to be clear — nobody handed that to me. I have taken and paid for courses before, and I never got what I needed out of it because I was never willing to put in the work until now.
It took hours, passion, determination, and above all, consistency. I also want to share that I have no coding experience, and honestly, I am not that smart of a person. What has led me to be successful is my consistency and determination. I think it's very important for everyone to share their successes as well as their failures so we can build and learn together.
