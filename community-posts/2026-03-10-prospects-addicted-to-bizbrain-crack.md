I can’t believe this is working — but it is.
Right now I have:
[ul][li]a rock band that wants to know where to invest marketing dollars to generate $X/month in passive income[li]a craft brewer trying to increase uptake of a slow-moving label[li]a cell phone distributor trying to regain B2C market share[li]an analytics firm that wants its data management processes QA’d and its value proposition repositioned from report generator to AI advisor[li]and a renovation company trying to automate lead generation and build estimates
For each of them I built a “BizBrain.”
Each BizBrain has a chat interface where the owner can essentially have a conversation with their business intelligence.
And the results have been… ridiculous.
BandBrain told the rock band to advertise in the UK and Germany, because Spotify and legislative royalty payouts per fan are higher [there.It](http://there.It) designed a strategy around a new metric focused on listener retention immediately after a release, predicted when they would reach their revenue target if they followed the plan, and spun up a live dashboard.
All of a sudden these stoner-rock musicians are completely obsessed with their data.
Meanwhile BeerBrain:
[ul][li]discovered inconsistencies between the brewery’s POS, accounting, tax, and inventory systems[li]identified about $6K/year in bookkeeping costs that exist purely to reconcile those inconsistencies[li]and found 200 cases of beer sitting in a store’s inventory system — “checked in” but never actually placed on shelves.
CellBrain built a marketing strategy to operate within newly legislated email opt-in rules, and helped them target the right segments inside what was basically an unruly jungle of customer data.
AnalyticsBrain:
[ul][li]caught calculation errors in reporting metrics[li]proposed improvements to their data engineering and semantic modeling[li]and designed a strategy to convert one-time dental patients into long-term patients.
